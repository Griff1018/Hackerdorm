## Question

Is P002 (Iced Lemon Tea 500ml) priced competitively against what Malaysian food outlets actually charge for iced lemon/lime tea, so we know whether the overstock is a demand problem or a purchasing-quantity problem?

## What we found

P002 sells at RM 4.50. Across five Malaysian outlet menus listed publicly on foodpanda on 2026-10-10, iced lemon/lime tea (Teh O Ais Limau / Air Limau / Iced Teh O Limau) sells between RM 2.40 and RM 4.70. RM 4.50 therefore sits in the upper part of the observed band, at the same level as the most expensive comparable drink found (Iced Teh O Limau, RM 4.70, Warung Selera Kak Sue). There is little headroom to raise the price.

Working hypothesis, not a proven causal conclusion: because P002 sold 206 units in the period, the most of any product, while holding 13.86 days of stock against a 10.00 day threshold, the overstock may be a purchasing-quantity problem rather than a demand problem. The next-order quantity (pack size 24, lead time 2 days) is therefore the first lever to test before discounting; this desk check does not establish customer demand or causality.

## How to use this

Use this as a hypothesis for the next test: change the order quantity first and compare the next matching period; do not treat the desk check as proof of a demand diagnosis. Re-check before any actual price change - five delivery-platform menus are a snapshot, not a market study.
